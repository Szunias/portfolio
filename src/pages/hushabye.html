HUSHABYE

A first-person horror game I am developing in Unity 6, played with a microphone: you hush a crying baby with your own voice and lure a blind creature away with recordings of it.

Watch the full game loop

A babysitter’s night in a house in late-1980s Europe. The bundle asleep in the crib looks like the baby on CAM 1, but the real baby is crying somewhere else in the house. Over five nights, find it, carry it back and swap it into the crib before the crying fades.

Single-player psychological horror, in production. 2026, PC. Unity 6 HDRP and C#, sound in Wwise. Latest video from version 7.2.65 (October 2026).

Hushing the baby, luring with your voice

A real “shh” into the microphone calms the baby in your arms: a calm baby cries more quietly, and its crying fades more slowly. Without a microphone, holding Space does the same, more weakly.

With the monitor raised, the player picks a camera, records their voice into it and plays it back from the speaker in that room. The creature goes to check. Every playback costs battery, and the baby’s crying is louder than any recording.

Recordings stay in memory on the player’s PC. The voice is never written to disk or sent anywhere.

A baby monitor from 1988

The monitor’s screen is drawn like the on-screen display of a VHS camcorder: a large CAM number, a strip of the six cameras, an LCD battery, REC with tape blocks, and a date stamp from October 1988.

There are no counters on screen. The baby’s state is heard rather than shown, and an action prompt is one key and one word.

The menu plays the same tape picture of the nursery behind a few plain words.

The creature in the nursery wall

The creature is a blind woman’s head on a procedural neck many metres long, with a headless body in a floral nightgown limping behind it. It hunts by sound.

It lives behind a patch of wallpaper next to the crib. The wall bulges and tears in strips and the head comes out first; on the way back the body goes in first and the head last.

It searches rooms and looks into hiding places; if it finds the player, the night ends. Later nights add a red ribbon for the crib and poppy seeds the creature stops to count, both folk protections against changelings.

Sound through rooms and doorways

Every zone of the house is a Wwise room and every doorway a portal, so a door left open lets the crying through. Before Wwise, I built a C# DSP engine that carried sound along the same graph of zones, with five FDN (feedback delay network) reverbs.

A voice played from a camera is processed in C# like the speaker of a cheap 1980s monitor: a 300–3400 Hz band, soft distortion, 9-bit grain, hiss, 50 Hz hum and short dropouts.

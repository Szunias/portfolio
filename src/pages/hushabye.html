HUSHABYE

A first-person horror game I am developing in Unity 6, played by singing into the microphone. On a trial night as a babysitter in 1980s–90s Europe, the player watches the nursery through the infrared HUSH-1 baby monitor, and what lies in the crib sleeps only while someone sings.

2026 · PC · Unity 6 HDRP · C# · Developed with AI assistance

How it plays

  1. Sing

    When TALK blinks green, hold it and sing into your microphone.

  2. Be still

    When the monitor goes dark and the breathing stops, it is listening: freeze and stay silent.

  3. Escape before six

    Get the front-door key and turn it in the lock before the parents come home at six.

What I built

Voice
YIN pitch detection runs in real time and only on the player’s PC; the voice is never sent anywhere. A phrase judge scores every note against the melody in the singer’s own key, which their first steady note sets.
DSP engine
A C# engine that carries sound through walls, floors and doorways along a graph of the house’s zones. Each zone type has one of five FDN reverbs: room, kitchen, corridor, tiled bathroom and a dead pantry.
Diegetic sound
The monitor’s speaker is a lo-fi mono chain with hiss; the house around the player is full band. A sound with hiss is far away, and one without it is right next to you.
Music box
It plays retuned glockenspiel samples from VSCO 2 Community Edition (CC0) with a shorter decay, a 450 Hz high-pass, teeth detuned by ±3 cents and the whirr of the mechanism.
Engine
The game runs in Unity 6 with HDRP and is written in C#.

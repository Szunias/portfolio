Testing whether AI-generated sound is fast enough for gameplay

2025–2026 · Solo researcher and prototype developer · Unreal Engine 5 · Python · 68-page thesis · Howest DAE

A thesis and a working Unreal prototype that measure how long a generated sound takes to reach the player after an in-game event.

I did the research and built the prototype: the Unreal client, the Python generation server, the cache and the fallback. The AI generators I tested were too slow to answer a player action, so the system prepares likely sounds early and falls back to simple synthesis.

How long each method took

I timed five methods, one procedural baseline and four AI configurations, from the in-game event to the first audible sample, five runs per method and prompt. The figures are 95th percentile and cover the whole wait in the prototype: request, generation or transfer, decoding and playback, not the model alone.

Method95th-percentile latency
Procedural DSP25 ms
MMAudio Small1.9 s
AudioGen2.8 s
MMAudio Large3.4 s
ElevenLabs3.5 s

The thesis works with a 100 to 150 ms budget for interactive sound effects, taken from earlier latency studies. Only the procedural baseline stays inside it. A separate quality check by one rater covered 60 anonymised samples. Test machine: AMD Ryzen 9 7940HS, RTX 5070 Laptop GPU with 8 GB VRAM, 64 GB RAM, Unreal Engine 5.4.4, Python 3.11.7.

Preparing the sound before the event

Because a first generation is too slow, the server watches the game state and prepares sounds the player is likely to trigger: approaching a door prepares door sounds, entering a forest prepares ambience. The result is cached and played on demand. When the guess is wrong, the game plays the synthetic fallback and keeps the generated sample for later.

The same prompt through each method

Four samples of one prompt, a heavy footstep on wet stone. Full results and the method are in the thesis.

  • Procedural DSP, the fallback: low latency, clearly synthetic.
  • AudioGen, for ambience: better texture, but the response lands seconds late.
  • MMAudio, for prepared content. The benchmark separates Small and Large; this sample is not used to compare them.
  • ElevenLabs, for pregeneration: often the cleanest result in this set, but not safe for an instant response.

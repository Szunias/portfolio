All four karts share one screen, which is what the mix has to stay readable through.

Engines that follow the kart

I was the only person on audio. I edited roughly 450 source files, built engines that react to each kart, recorded the character voices with the team and mixed the game so it stays readable with four players on one screen.

Every kart has its own engine sound, and it follows that kart’s speed, throttle and load. Fast control changes are smoothed so the pitch does not jump abruptly.

The trailer, with the final engines, power-ups, collisions, voices and mix.

Making a pile-up readable

The first mix gave every engine and effect the same priority, so pile-ups became unreadable. I split the sounds into three priority tiers and added concurrency limits: kart feedback, major crashes and pickups stay audible, and distant engines and less important sounds yield first.

Voices recorded in the last week

In the final week I recorded five teammates performing reactions for the four characters. The game varies the recordings and leaves time between them, so the same lines do not repeat constantly.

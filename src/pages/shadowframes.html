Shadow Frames

A horror game set in one abandoned pub, where the player finds changes by ear. As sole audio designer, I built the sounds that announce and locate each change.

Sound design and implementation

I handled sound design, voice recording, implementation and mixing. The finished game uses over 200 Wwise events, with mix changes driven by the player’s actions and the enemy’s distance.

The project brought together students from Howest DAE, RITCS and the Royal Conservatory of Ghent.

Alert, localisation and confirmation

The player finds changes in the pub without on-screen markers. Each anomaly has an alert, a directional clue and a confirmation sound.

Mix priority during an anomaly

The mix reacts to the enemy’s distance, the raised camera and which anomalies are active. When too many sounds play at once, music and ambience give way. The clues, the camera and the enemy stay, because those are the sounds the player is acting on.

Fixes from weekly playtests

The enemy sound switched on and off near one particular distance. Footsteps failed when parts of the level moved. And raising the Polaroid camera was easy to miss.

I stopped the enemy sound from flickering, made footsteps follow the actual floor material, and gave the raised camera its own mix state.

Every confirmed photograph goes on the board.

Audio settings in Unreal and Wwise

I exposed floor, anomaly and enemy settings as Unreal Data Assets, so designers could tune them without changing code. Wwise holds the bus hierarchy and the curves that control the mix.

Every sound in the pub was mine

I was the only person on audio. I made the sounds that announce each change and lead the player to it, recorded the voice acting, then implemented and mixed all of it as 200+ Wwise events in Unreal.

A collaboration between Howest DAE, RITCS and the Royal Conservatory of Ghent.

The gameplay recording with its spatial mix. Headphones help.

Three sounds find one anomaly

Every change in the pub is announced, located and confirmed by ear. The three frames are the three moments, in the order the player meets them.

What the mix drops first

The mix reacts to the enemy’s distance, the raised camera and which anomalies are active. When too many sounds play at once, music and ambience give way. The clues, the camera and the enemy stay, because those are the sounds the player is acting on.

Three things the weekly tests kept catching

The enemy sound switched on and off near one particular distance. Footsteps failed when parts of the level moved. And raising the Polaroid camera was easy to miss.

I stopped the enemy sound from flickering, made footsteps follow the actual floor material, and gave the raised camera its own mix state.

Every confirmed photograph goes on the board.

What the designers could change without me

Floor, anomaly and enemy settings are exposed in Unreal, so the designers could tune them without changing code.

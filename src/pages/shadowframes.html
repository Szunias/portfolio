Shadow Frames

Sole audio designer on a horror game navigated entirely by sound.

No HUD markers. No minimap. The player finds every anomaly by following sound through an abandoned Irish pub.

Sole Audio Designer on a multidisciplinary team · 4 months · Howest DAE + RITCS + Royal Conservatory of Ghent

Overview

Ten anomalies with no HUD markers

An elderly man with dementia returns to a black-and-white pub where fragments of memory appear as moving glasses, turning chairs and wandering shadows. The player finds each anomaly by ear, photographs it and pins it to an investigation board.

I was the only audio designer on the team: cues for 10 anomaly types, sound editing in Reaper, systems in Wwise and UE5, the mix, and a fresh playtest every week.

The core system

How you find something you cannot see

A glass starts moving behind the bar while you are standing in another room.

A stinger cuts through the ambience: something changed, but not what or where. Turn, and the scrape of glass gives you a direction. Closer in, a loop around that one glass becomes easy to place — and it stops the moment you take the photograph.

What you hear Stinger → scrape from the bar → loop around the glass → the camera confirms the shot.

Challenges

Playtests exposed distance-state flutter

Enemy distance

Tension flickered when the enemy stood at the edge of its range.

One distance line switched the threat layer on and off, so an enemy hovering on it made the mix stutter several times a second. I replaced it with a buffer zone: the layer starts when the enemy comes close and stops only after a clear retreat.

Footsteps

Every level edit could break the floor sounds.

Hand-placed audio tags went stale whenever a designer moved part of the floor. The game now reads the physical material under the player, so footsteps follow level changes automatically.

The Polaroid

The camera looked risky, but it did not sound risky.

Raising the camera narrows your view, so the pub goes muffled, breathing moves forward and your hands rustle close to the ear. A reversed whisper confirms the shot.

Production

Making the audio easy for the team to change

Surface, anomaly and enemy settings live in Unreal DataAssets, so designers tuned the game without asking me to change code.

The mix runs on rules tied to enemy distance, camera state and anomaly activity — not one-off volume changes placed around the level.

The brief

The player had to listen before they could move.

An elderly man with dementia returns to an abandoned Irish pub. Memories appear as anomalies: a chair scraping across a room, glass moving behind the bar, a whisper coming from the corridor. There are no HUD markers pointing to them. The player finds each one by ear, photographs it and adds it to an investigation board.

I joined the team as the only audio designer. That meant owning the whole path from source material to the final build: finding sounds, editing and mixing them, writing the Wwise and Unreal integration, and bringing a playable update to the team every week.

Four months at the audio desk

How the work actually happened

Most days started with Soundly or Envato open on one monitor and Reaper on the other. The same four-step loop carried the project from rough scenes to a playable mix.

01

Find the raw material

I searched Soundly and Envato Elements, auditioning source recordings until I found material with the right age, weight and room tone for the pub.

02

Cut and shape it in Reaper

I trimmed, layered, pitched and mixed the sources in Reaper. Library files became specific objects: this chair, this camera, this floorboard, in this room.

03

Build it into the game

I moved the assets into Wwise, wrote the UE5 gameplay audio code and created DataAssets so designers could tune footsteps, anomalies and enemy behaviour without waiting for me.

04

Play it for the team

Once a week we played the latest build and collected feedback. If a cue was unclear, I went back to the Reaper session; if it fired at the wrong moment, I went back to the implementation.

What had to work

Four systems the player could hear

The technical work mattered only when it changed a decision: where to walk, when to hide, whether the photo worked.

01

Navigation

Anomalies point to themselves

A short spawn alert stops the player, a directional clue identifies the room, and a unique loop leads them to the exact object. No marker is needed.

02

Movement

Four floor materials, one reusable system

A downward physics trace reads the Physical Material and maps it to a Wwise Switch. Designers could add and tune surfaces in-editor without new audio code.

03

Threat

Enemy tension without boundary stutter

The first proximity mix flickered when the enemy crossed one threshold. I replaced it with hysteresis: enter at 70%, leave at 60%.

04

Camera

The Polaroid changes the whole mix

Raising the camera applies a 3kHz low-pass, brings up breathing and arms the shutter sequence. A reversed whisper confirms a successful capture.

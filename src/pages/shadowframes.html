Shadow Frames

Sole audio designer: sound design, voice recording, implementation and the mix, in Wwise and Unreal Engine 5.

Watch with sound Trailer on YouTube ↗

A black-and-white horror game set in one abandoned pub. The player finds changes by ear and photographs them with a Polaroid camera.

2025, four months, at Howest DAE with students from RITCS and the Royal Conservatory of Ghent. Over 200 Wwise events in the finished game. Tools: Wwise, Unreal Engine 5, C++, REAPER.

The ambience follows the board

Every confirmed photograph goes on the board and moves the pub’s ambience one stage further. The first adds weird noises to the base layer and the third a ghostly screech. The fourth swaps both for a horror drone, and from the eighth only the horror background ambience is left.

In Wwise the board is a switch group on RTPC_Photos_OnBoard, with a stage for every count from 0 to 10. In SC_AMB_Progressive, a layer that two stages share keeps playing through the switch instead of restarting.

Anomaly sounds and Keiran’s voice lines

With no on-screen markers, each anomaly reaches the player in four sounds.

  1. Alert

    A short alert with no direction signals that an object in the pub has changed.

  2. Object

    The changed object plays its own spatial sound, and the player follows it through the pub.

  3. Camera

    Raising the camera lowers the pub ambience and brings breathing and hand movement forward, in a mix state of its own.

  4. Whisper

    A reversed whisper confirms the shot.

DA_Anomaly_Global holds the shared anomaly events and gives every anomaly Blueprint its own AnomalyType switch value. The Data Asset ↗

I recorded the voice lines for Keiran, the player character. His lines for the story anomalies play from one switch container on the StoryAnomaly switch group. Four reminder lines, such as IshouldPickup, work the same way on a switch group of their own.

An Unreal Data Table ↗, DT_Dialogue, lists the duration of each line.

Footsteps and the enemy

I exposed floor, anomaly and enemy settings as Unreal Data Assets, so designers could tune them without changing code.

The player’s footsteps change with the floor: tiles, wood, concrete, creaky wood and creaky wooden stairs. BP_FootstepAudioManager listens to the On Footstep event, sets the FootstepSurface switch for the surface each step reports, then posts Play_PLYR_Footstep. The enemy’s footsteps cover the same five surfaces from a Data Asset of their own ↗.

DA_Enemy_Presence runs dread layers on RTPC_Enemy_Proximity (max distance 700, min 100) and a breathing loop on the player’s stress level. The layers start at one threshold (5.0) and stop at a lower one (2.0), so they hold steady while the enemy hovers near the edge.

The mix also follows which anomalies are active. When too many sounds play at once, music and ambience give way so the clues, the camera and the enemy stay clear.

AudioQ

AudioQ is a sampler plug-in I built on my own in C++ with JUCE. It plays a recording as a loop, in random regions or in short grains, and sends it through filters, a compressor and four effects I wrote: tremolo, bitcrusher, stutter and reverse.

The recording shows the version from before I remade it: I play a file through the tremolo, the filters and the compressor, switch to the random and granular modes, and loop a short region drawn on the waveform.

From the file player to the output

The interface groups the controls into panels, but the audio passes through them in one fixed order. The filters and the compressor are JUCE modules. I wrote the file player, the make-up gain after the compressor and everything from the tremolo on.

Source AudioFilePlayer

Drop a WAV, AIFF or MP3 on the plug-in and drag across the waveform to set the loop region. The tempo knob resamples the file, so speed and pitch change together. Random and granular modes keep jumping to new regions of the file.

double minLen = isRandom ? 0.1 : 0.05;
double maxLen = isRandom ? 3.0 : 0.20;

Filters juce::dsp::StateVariableTPTFilter

A low-pass and a high-pass filter from JUCE’s DSP module. I skewed both cutoff ranges so the low frequencies get more of the knob’s travel: halfway round is about 2 kHz.

juce::NormalisableRange<float>(20.0f, 20000.0f, 1.0f, 0.3f)

Compressor juce::dsp::Compressor

JUCE’s compressor, followed by a make-up gain I added so that compression stays audible. The gain is worked out from the threshold and the ratio and capped at 18 dB.

float makeupDb = juce::jlimit(0.0f, 18.0f,
    (-cThresh) * (1.0f - (1.0f / juce::jmax(1.0f, cRatio))) * 0.35f);

Tremolo

My tremolo squares its sine LFO, so the level spends longer in the dips. From a depth of 0.5 the dips reach silence.

float shapedLfo = lfoVal * lfoVal;
float minGain = juce::jmax(0.0f, 1.0f - (tremDepth * 2.0f));

Bitcrusher

My bitcrusher rounds each sample to steps of 1/2bits. Downsample then holds each value for up to 40 samples, as if the sample rate dropped by that factor.

crushHeldSample[ch] = std::round(data[s] * levels) / levels;

Stutter

The plug-in keeps the last second of audio in a history buffer. When stutter is on, it copies the most recent chunk out of that buffer and repeats it. A chunk lasts one period of the stutter rate.

int targetLen = juce::jmax(64, (int)(currentSR / (double)stutterRate));

Reverse

Reverse reads the same history buffer backwards. It plays the most recent 180 ms in reverse, then takes the next 180 ms.

reverseLength = juce::jmax(1, (int)(sampleRate * 0.18));

Dry/wet

A dry copy of the signal is saved straight after the file player, before the filters, and blended back in here.

wet[s] = dry[s] * (1.0f - dryWet) + wet[s] * dryWet;

Output

Gain comes last, up to about +10 dB. A safety check then clears any block that peaks above 4.0, about +12 dB, and shows a warning. What passes goes to the live output display.

if (peakLevel > 4.0f)
{
    dangerousVolumeDetected.store(true);
    buffer.clear();
    return;
}

The chain follows the source on GitHub, updated in July 2026. The download there is still v1.0.0 for Windows, VST3, and I have not yet tested AudioQ across hosts, operating systems, sample rates and channel layouts.

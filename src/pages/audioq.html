AudioQ

AudioQ is a sampler plug-in I built on my own in C++ with JUCE. It plays a recording as a loop, in random regions or in short grains, and sends it through filters, a compressor and four effects I wrote: tremolo, bitcrusher, stutter and reverse.

A demo of the earlier version

The recording shows the version from before I remade it: I play a file through the tremolo, the filters and the compressor, switch to the random and granular modes, and loop a short region drawn on the waveform.

From the file player to the output

The panels group the controls, but the audio passes through them in this fixed order. The filters and the compressor are JUCE modules. I wrote the file player, the make-up gain and everything from the tremolo on.

  1. Source

    Loops a region dragged on the waveform, or jumps between regions in random and granular modes. AudioFilePlayer

  1. Filters

    Low-pass and high-pass, skewed so halfway round the knob is about 2 kHz. juce::dsp::StateVariableTPTFilter

  2. Compressor

    Then a make-up gain I added, worked out from threshold and ratio, capped at 18 dB. juce::dsp::Compressor

    float makeupDb = juce::jlimit(0.0f, 18.0f,
    (-cThresh) * (1.0f - (1.0f / juce::jmax(1.0f, cRatio))) * 0.35f);

  1. Tremolo

    A squared sine LFO, so the level stays longer in the dips. From depth 0.5 they reach silence.

  2. Bitcrusher

    Rounds each sample to steps of 1/2bits. Downsample holds it for up to 40 samples.

  3. Stutter

    Repeats the latest chunk of a one-second history buffer, one period of the stutter rate long.

  4. Reverse

    Reads the same buffer backwards, 180 ms at a time.

  5. Dry/wet

    Blends in a dry copy taken straight after the file player, before the filters.

  1. Output

    Gain up to about +10 dB. A safety check then clears any block peaking above 4.0, about +12 dB, and shows a warning.

    if (peakLevel > 4.0f)
{
    dangerousVolumeDetected.store(true);
    buffer.clear();
    return;
}

The chain follows the source on GitHub, updated in July 2026. The download there is still v1.0.0, and I have not yet tested AudioQ across hosts, operating systems, sample rates and channel layouts.

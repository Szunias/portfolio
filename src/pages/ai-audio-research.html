AI Audio Research

A thesis and a working Unreal prototype that test whether AI-generated sound is fast enough for gameplay: how long a generated sound takes to reach the player after an in-game event.

Solo researcher and prototype developer · Unreal Engine 5 · Python · 68-page thesis

How long each method took

I did the research and built the prototype: the Unreal client, the Python generation server, the cache and the fallback.

I timed five methods, one procedural baseline and four AI configurations, from the in-game event to the first audible sample, five runs per method and prompt. The figures cover the whole wait: request, generation or transfer, decoding and playback, not the model alone.

The thesis works with a 100 to 150 ms budget for interactive sound effects, taken from earlier latency studies. Only the procedural baseline stays inside it. A separate quality check by one rater covered 60 anonymised samples.

Preparing the sound before the event

Because a first generation is too slow, the server watches the game state and prepares sounds the player is likely to trigger: approaching a door prepares door sounds, entering a forest prepares ambience. The result is cached and played on demand.

When the guess is wrong, the game plays the synthetic fallback and keeps the generated sample for later.

Score, sound design and mix for a short film about an earthquake.

Watch the film (0:44)

I made every sound effect myself, from Zoom H6 recordings I took on walks through the city and from synthesisers, without any sound libraries.

2024, a school assignment: the exam project for my DAW 2 course, a 44-second film. REAPER, Zoom H6, Vital, ReaSynth, TAL-NoiseMaker.

Cue by cue

  1. 0:03The rumble

    A low rumble swells under the plain, seven seconds before the ground splits.

  2. 0:07The craft

    The craft races across the flats, and the main theme comes in under its engines.

  3. 0:10The ground splits

    The earth cracks over the rumble as the fault runs across the plain.

  4. 0:18The collapse

    The ridge bursts apart behind the craft, and the low end is at its heaviest in the film.

  5. 0:28Closing gate

    A gate shuts on the valley, then the sound drops to almost nothing for three seconds.

  6. 0:37Title hit

    The title lands on a single hit, over the last bars of the theme.

How I made the sounds

Stretched door creaks, pallet impacts and concrete scrapes from my recordings became the materials breaking above the rumble. Vital, ReaSynth and TAL-NoiseMaker gave me the drones, risers and alarms, layered with field recordings.

I automated volume, panning and reverb sends to move the engines and whooshes with the picture, and recreated the Doppler effect where a sound needed it.

The session ran at 48 kHz / 24-bit, and I checked the final mix with a loudness meter against the level the course required.

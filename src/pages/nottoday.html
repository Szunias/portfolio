Not Today, Darling!

A couch kart racer for up to four players, where grannies on mobility scooters race over a course built from household objects in a living room. I was the sole audio designer: I built the engines in MetaSounds and the sounds for pickups, power-ups and zones, recorded the team as the grannies, and mixed it all for one shared screen.

MetaSounds engines and pickups

Every kart has its own engine sound, controlled by speed, throttle and load. I smoothed fast control changes so the pitch follows the kart without jumping abruptly.

Pickups and power-ups share one MetaSound patch, MSP_MonoArray.

Each time the patch plays, it takes a file from its array, never the same one twice in a row, at a random pitch between Pitch Min and Pitch Max. MS_Powerups feeds it CreamPiePickupSounds and also sends the result to the AB_Ducker audio bus.

One mix for a shared screen

Up to four players share one screen. I sorted the sounds into three priority tiers with concurrency limits, so kart feedback, major crashes and pickups stay audible in a pile-up, and distant engines and minor sounds give way first. A zone plays its sound once, when the first granny enters, and fades out when the last one leaves. The commentator ducks the rest of the mix, so the item names stay clear.

The grannies, voiced by the team

I recorded the team performing the grannies. Each one has a line when she is picked and another when she wins. The game varies the recordings and leaves time between reactions to limit repetition.

Transientica

A rhythm game you play by beatboxing into a microphone. I am developing it solo in Unity 6 and wrote its audio recognition in C#: the game learns how each player makes a kick, a snare and a hi-hat, then reads every live hit as an input on one of three lanes.

2025–present · Solo · Unity 6 · C# · KNN · FFT · In development

From a sound to a lane

The player records at least ten examples of each sound with their own microphone, and the classifier learns from those recordings. They are saved as labelled features in JSON, so training can be updated between rounds.

I wrote the recognition in C#, including the FFT and the KNN classifier, so the audio processing runs in the same application as the game. Every hit during a song takes the same path:

  1. OnsetThe microphone registers a hit.
  2. FFT512 samples.
  3. FeaturesSeven features are measured from the hit.
  4. KNNThe classifier compares them with the player’s own recordings.
  5. LaneKick Snare Hi-hat

Judged on the audio clock

Each hit is matched to the nearest note on Unity’s audio clock and scored by its timing. A short calibration estimates the microphone delay, and the offset can be adjusted in 1 ms steps.

Around the recognition I built the rest of the game: song selection, beatmaps, scoring and saved results.

The 2025 Python prototype

I started with a prototype in 2025: a Python script recognised each sound and sent the result to Unity over OSC. For the current build I moved recognition into C#, so the audio processing and gameplay run in one application.

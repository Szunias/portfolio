Transientica

A rhythm game you play by beatboxing. I am developing it solo in Unity 6 and wrote its audio recognition in C#.

Watch the demo with sound

The game learns how each player makes a kick, a snare and a hi‑hat, then reads every live hit as an input on one of three lanes.

2025 to now, solo. Unity 6, C#. The gameplay is from the March 2026 build.

From a sound to a lane

Before the first song, the player records at least ten kicks, snares and hi‑hats with their own microphone. The game measures every recording and saves the measurements, labelled by sound, in a JSON file, so a player can retrain between rounds.

During a song, every hit the microphone registers goes through a 512‑sample FFT. Seven numbers are measured from it, the same seven saved for each training recording, and the recordings with the closest numbers decide which sound it was.

The game names the sound at the top of the screen, with the classifier’s confidence under it, and the hit becomes an input on that lane.

The FFT and the k‑nearest‑neighbours (KNN) classifier are my own C# code, so the recognition runs inside the game with no Python beside it.

Judged on the audio clock

Each hit is matched to the nearest note on Unity’s audio clock and scored by its timing.

A short calibration estimates the microphone delay, and the offset can be adjusted in 1 ms steps.

Around the recognition I built the rest of the game: song selection, beatmaps, scoring and saved results.

The 2025 Python prototype

The first version was a Python script. It found each hit with onset detection, classified it with a machine learning model I had trained on three beatbox sounds (a boom for the kick, a sharp ch for the snare, a crisp ts for the hi‑hat) and sent the result to Unity over OSC.

The current build is the game I planned at the end of the prototype’s video: a rhythm game where you beatbox in sync with the beat to score points.

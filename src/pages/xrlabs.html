The requirement

A sound behind a wall must stay behind the wall.

In a training simulation, spatial audio is not decoration. The trainee may hear a threat before they can see it, so the mix has to communicate where it is, how far away it is and what stands between them.

I build that path in Unity with Steam Audio. Sources react to the virtual room geometry instead of playing as clean sounds with a volume fade. A closed door, a corner and an open room need to produce three different decisions.

  1. 01Sourcevoice, gunshot or movement
  2. 02Geometrywall, door, corner or open room
  3. 03Propagationdirection, distance and occlusion
  4. 04Decisionreact before the threat is visible

What the trainee needs to hear

One cue has to answer three questions.

If any answer is wrong, the trainee builds the wrong picture of the room. I treat each as a functional part of the simulation, not a separate polish pass.

01

Direction

Where did it happen?

The source has to remain stable in 3D space while the trainee turns, moves and shares the environment with other users.

02

Distance

How far away is it?

Attenuation and reflections need to separate a nearby room from the far end of a corridor without flattening every cue into the same loudness.

03

Obstruction

What is between us?

A wall or closed door changes more than level. Filtering and propagation have to preserve the sense of separation from the source.

React without a floating prompt

The goal is not a dramatic mix. It is a spatial cue clear enough to support a training decision while remaining part of the scene.

Engineering decisions

Three ways spatial audio can stop being trustworthy.

The hard part is not making a sound impressive in isolation. It is keeping its behaviour believable while rooms, scenarios and shared Unity scenes keep changing.

Door≠ volume fader

Problem

Quieter still sounded like the same room.

Reducing gain alone can make a voice softer, but it does not place the voice behind a closed door or around a corner.

Decision

I use Steam Audio propagation and occlusion against the actual virtual geometry, then tune the result in the scene. The obstacle changes the spectrum and path, not only the number on a volume control.

No markeraudio carries the cue

Problem

A UI arrow would invalidate the exercise.

When visibility is limited, revealing a threat through an interface prompt removes the need to interpret the environment.

Decision

I place and test voices, gunshots and movement cues against the room layout so direction and distance remain readable from the headset. The information stays diegetic.

One projectmany contributors

Problem

Spatial behaviour had to survive the next sync.

Audio settings and geometry live inside Unity scenes shared by designers and engineers. Unstructured changes are easy to lose or break in version control.

Decision

I keep the implementation and scene changes repeatable within the Perforce workflow, so the team can sync updates without silently replacing the spatial setup.

A necessary boundary

Public principle. Private scenario.

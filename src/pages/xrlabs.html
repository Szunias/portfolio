The short version

I handle audio on selected Unity simulations where trainees rely on direction, distance and the room itself to understand what is happening. Steam Audio uses the virtual walls and doors, and the important cues work without floating markers.

The job

Sound that has to support a real decision

In a training simulation, a trainee may hear a threat before they see it. The sound has to communicate where it came from, how far away it is and whether a wall or door stands between them.

I build that behaviour in Unity with Steam Audio. Instead of making every distant source simply quieter, I let voices, gunshots and movement react to the shape of the virtual facility.

What the trainee experiences

You hear a voice somewhere beyond the room. When you turn your head, it stays fixed in place. As you move closer, the distance becomes easier to judge; when a door closes, the voice becomes muffled and indirect. You can react before the person comes into view.

Inside the headset

What the room has to tell you

A cue only works when its direction, distance and obstruction agree. If one feels wrong, the trainee builds the wrong picture of the space.

Picture a gunshot at the far end of a corridor, followed by movement behind a closed door.

The gunshot must stay in the same position while the trainee turns or moves. Its reflections and level help separate the far end of the corridor from the next room.

The movement behind the door needs a different character: softer high frequencies, less direct sound and a path that feels blocked. A volume fade alone would still make it sound as if both sources were standing in the same open space.

What changes in the headset The source stays fixed → distance changes the balance → walls and doors reshape the sound → the trainee decides where to look.

In production

What had to keep working as the project changed

A convincing demo is not enough. The audio has to survive new rooms, changing scenarios and a Unity project shared by several disciplines.

Doors and corners

Making a voice quieter did not make it sound like it was in another room.

The first shortcut was a simple volume reduction. It softened the voice, but the direct, clear sound still placed the speaker in the same room as the trainee.

I used Steam Audio to read the actual geometry, then tuned the result in the scene. A closed door now removes clarity and redirects the voice, while an open doorway allows a more direct path.

No interface arrow

Showing the answer on screen would defeat the exercise.

When visibility is limited, a marker can reveal a threat before the trainee has interpreted the environment. That removes the listening decision the scenario is meant to test.

I place and test voices, gunshots and movement against the actual room layout until their direction and distance remain readable in the headset. The information stays inside the scene.

Shared Unity scenes

A scene update could quietly overwrite the spatial setup.

Audio settings and geometry live in scenes touched by designers and engineers. Without a consistent structure, a normal sync or merge can remove part of the setup without an obvious error.

I keep the audio changes organised and repeatable in Perforce, so the team can update shared scenes without silently losing the spatial behaviour.

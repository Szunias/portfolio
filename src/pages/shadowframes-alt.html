Shadow Frames

2025 · Sole audio designer · Wwise · Unreal Engine 5 · C++ · REAPER · 4 months · Howest DAE with RITCS and the Royal Conservatory of Ghent

A horror game in Unreal Engine 5, set in one abandoned pub. Ten things in the pub change over the course of the game and the player has to find each of them by ear. There are no markers on screen.

I was the only person on audio. I made the sounds that announce a change and lead the player to it, recorded the voice acting, and implemented and mixed all of it in Wwise and Unreal, a little over 200 events by the end.

How a change is heard

Each anomaly is three sounds. A short alert with no direction says that something has changed. Then the changed object starts making a directional sound of its own, so the player can walk towards it. When the player raises the Polaroid camera and photographs the object, a closer confirmation plays.

What the playtests changed

We playtested every week, and three things came back often enough to fix. The enemy’s sound switched on and off when the player stood near one particular distance from it, so I stopped it flickering. Footsteps failed when parts of the level moved, so I made them follow the actual floor material under the player. And raising the Polaroid camera was easy to miss, so I made the mix react to it: the pub gets quieter and the player’s breathing and hand movement come forward. A reversed whisper confirms each photograph.

What the designers could change without me

Floor, anomaly and enemy settings are Data Assets in Unreal, so the designers could tune them without touching code. The mix listens to the enemy’s distance, the raised camera and which anomalies are active. When too many sounds play at once, music and ambience give way first; the clues, the camera and the enemy, the sounds that affect play, are the last to go.

# Portfolio Copy Rules

1. The first paragraph states what the project is, my exact role and one concrete contribution.
2. Use a structure that fits the project. Never reuse a mandatory case-study template.
3. Keep featured pages to 3–6 meaningful sections. Archive pages should be shorter.
4. Do not use snapshots, stats grids, fact sidebars or challenge cards by default.
5. State each metric once, with its version, scope and measurement context.
6. Do not manufacture a “human” voice with fragments, rhetorical questions, casual connectors, dramatic one-line endings or LinkedIn-style punchlines.
7. Put proof next to the claim: gameplay, profiler, diagram, code, before/after audio or a table.
8. Headings name the actual content. Avoid Overview, Challenges, Solution, Features, What I learned, The core system and The short version.
9. A border or background is for interactive media, data, code or warnings, not ordinary prose.
10. Describe limitations plainly. Do not turn every failed attempt into an inspirational lesson.
11. Read the page aloud. Delete any sentence written to impress rather than inform.
12. Before publishing, compare every technical claim with the public code, CV and project page.

## Voice

- Direct and understated.
- Use specific tools, constraints and decisions.
- Prefer a number or visible proof to an adjective.
- Credit collaborators and state the exact role.
- Keep one memorable human detail when it helps; do not build the whole page around it.

## Layout

- Open hero without a prose card.
- One flat metadata line.
- Full-width gameplay, screenshot, diagram, waveform or code beside the relevant claim.
- Thin separators and whitespace instead of repeated containers.
- Use `details` for genuinely secondary material.
- Archive pages: 180–350 words. Flagship pages: 4–6 sections. Research pages may be longer when the method requires it.

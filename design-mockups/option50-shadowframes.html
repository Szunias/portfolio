The Project

The Game

Every semester at DAE we vote on project ideas from game design students. This was second year, second semester, four month project. Shadow Frames was a horror pitch and I wanted in.

It's a psychological horror about an old man with dementia wandering through an abandoned 1960s Irish pub, all in black and white. Anomalies keep showing up - levitating glasses, spinning chairs, drifting shadows - and each one is a fragment of some memory he can't let go of. At the end he finds his son hooked up to an IV, drinking, and the game just stops. You never find out what happens.

There's no HUD, no markers, nothing on screen telling you where to go. Sound is the only way to find anomalies. Mute the game and you're blind. Designed for headphones - HRTF binaural so you actually hear where things are in space. No music during exploration, just the pub: wood settling, clock ticking, wind through gaps. When music shows up, something's actually happening.

Multi-school collaboration: game devs at Howest DAE, a writer from RITCS, and a composer from the KASK Conservatory. I wrote a 49-page Sound Bible and built the dynamic music system from the composer's stems.

Technical

Audio Systems

Footstep Detection

The pub has wood floors, tiles, concrete, and creaky boards. A physics line-trace fires downward each step. The Physical Material from the hit maps to a Wwise Switch. Designers add new surfaces in the editor without touching code.

Enemy Proximity

There's always an enemy somewhere in the pub. As it gets closer, dread ambience and stressed breathing fade in through RTPC. Hysteresis state machine with dual thresholds (start 70%, stop 60%) so the audio doesn't flicker at the boundary.

Anomaly System

Three stages: 0.5s alert stinger on spawn, 1-2s directional hint (chair scraping, glass clinking - room-specific so you know which direction), then a sustained presence loop until you photograph it. Every anomaly sounds different. Spatial audio is the only way to find them.

Camera System

Raise the Polaroid and audio filters to 3kHz - tunnel hearing, breathing fills your ears. Take the shot: shutter click, flash pop with capacitor whine, film advance whirr. Capture something supernatural and you hear a reversed whisper. Run out of film and it's a hollow click. Reload takes 5 seconds where you can't move, and the pub doesn't pause for you.

The Project

The Concept

Can you play a rhythm game with your mouth? That's what I set out to test. Beatbox a kick, snare, or hi-hat, and the game responds in real-time.

I built the audio analysis pipeline in Python - spectral-flux onset detection feeding into an SVM classifier - and connected it to Unity over UDP/OSC. The classifier trains on your voice specifically: give it 30 examples of each drum sound and it learns your beatbox style. Different mic, different person, it still works because it calibrates to you.

The hard constraint was latency. A rhythm game with 180ms delay is unplayable. I got it down to 45ms at 92% accuracy across 2000+ test samples. The trick was keeping everything small: 512-sample FFT window, lock-free ringbuffer between threads, lightweight SVM that classifies in 2ms.

Technical

How It Works

Low Latency Pipeline

Under 60ms. That's the ceiling for a rhythm game to feel playable. Small FFT buffers (512 samples), minimal feature extraction, and UDP for OSC keep it there.

Learns Your Voice

Everyone beatboxes differently. The system trains on your voice and your microphone specifically, so accuracy stays high. Give it 30 examples per sound class, then play.

Split Architecture

Python handles audio analysis and ML inference. Unity handles gameplay. They talk over UDP/OSC, so I could restart one without touching the other. Made debugging way easier.

Challenges

What Went Wrong & How I Fixed It

Getting Latency Under 50ms

What Was Hard

First version had 180ms lag. Completely unplayable for a rhythm game.

What I Did

Shrunk the FFT window, added a circular buffer, swapped to a lightweight SVM, switched to UDP for OSC. 45ms at 92% accuracy. Done.

Kick vs Snare Confusion

What Was Hard

Both have sharp transients and broadband noise. The classifier couldn't tell them apart reliably.

What I Did

Added spectral centroid, zero-crossing rate, and envelope shape as features. Built separate models for solo hits vs patterns. That pushed accuracy from 78% to 92%.

No Audio Dropouts

What Was Hard

Heavy ML processing caused buffer underruns on slower machines. Audio would just cut out.

What I Did

Separated threads with a lock-free ringbuffer. Audio never blocks. ML runs on its own worker thread.

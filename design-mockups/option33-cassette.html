I spent 12 years studying classical piano, but I was always more curious about why sound works than how to perform it. That curiosity took me from music school through physics and maths, and eventually into game audio.

Now I design sounds and write the C++ and Python tools that make them run in real-time. I study at Howest DAE in Belgium, specializing in sound integration — Wwise, FMOD, Unreal Engine, the whole pipeline.

Production Notes How I Work

Approach

Note 01
Mix Hierarchy
In Shadow Frames, the pub ambience kept drowning out enemy footsteps. Instead of just pulling the volume down, I built a sidechain system that ducks the environment whenever an enemy moves. The threat always cuts through.
Note 02
Budgets & Constraints
Not Today Darling had 4 players on a shared screen with a tight budget. Voice count was blowing up. I got ruthless — aggressive priority culling, forced mono on anything non-critical. We shipped a clean mix that ran smooth.
Note 03
Parametric Sound Design
I hate doing the same work twice. Instead of making 20 unique engine sounds, I built one MetaSound graph that morphs based on speed and RPM. It covers walking, running, and sprinting from a single asset.
Note 04
Playtest-Driven Audio
Designers should not have to ask me to tweak a volume slider. In Shadow Frames, I exposed all key audio variables as DataAssets so the team could adjust tension curves and footstep volumes right in the editor. I focus on the code.

The Projects

Boney Boogaloo

Wwise v2021 Windows 2 weeks

Problem:

Zombie wave shooter. 50 zombies on screen. Player firing constantly.

Hit play and half the sounds disappear.

The engine can only play 256 sounds at once. Once they're all busy, new sounds get cut. Important stuff like zombie spawns and low ammo warnings were getting killed.

Also needed variation - same gunshot 100 times sounds robotic.

Fix:

Made the middleware pick randomly from 6 different gunshot files every time you shoot. Same for zombie sounds.

Set up quiet sounds to pause when things get busy, then resume when there's room. Important sounds never get cut.

Trimmed dead air from every audio file. Half-second of silence at the start of a reload? Cut. Files smaller and faster to load.

What went wrong:

First tried auto-cutting anything quieter than -80 dB. Figured players won't notice.

Wrong. Zombie spawn sounds start quiet then ramp up - they were getting cut mid-spawn. Players couldn't hear threats behind them.

Fixed it by marking spawn sounds as high priority and letting background music duck instead.

Ellen

Wwise v2021 Windows 3 weeks

Problem:

Platformer with magic combat. First time setting up spatial audio.

Sounds kept playing at max volume no matter where the player was. Reverb zones weren't switching when you walked between areas.

Everything sounded flat.

Fix:

Set up attenuation curves for each sound type. Footsteps drop off fast (2m), magic attacks carry further (8m).

Added Game Syncs that detect which room you're in and swap reverb settings. Small cave = tight reverb, big hall = long tail.

Hooked up player velocity to breathing sounds - running makes you breathe harder.

What went wrong:

First playtest, testers said "why does everything echo?"

Had reverb set too high trying to make it sound spacious. Turns out subtle works better - 15% wet instead of 40%.

Took 4 playtests to find the right balance.

Stomach Aches

Wwise 2024 4 Platforms 3 weeks

Problem:

FPS platformer inside a human body. Three zones - Brain, Mouth, Stomach. Needed to run on Android phones with 2GB RAM.

Audio memory budget on cheap Android: 15 MB max. Load all three zones and you're at 25+ MB. Game crashes on launch.

Windows and Mac handle uncompressed audio fine, but Android needs every byte optimized.

Fix:

Only load audio for the zone you're in. Start in Brain? Load Brain sounds. Walk into Mouth? Unload Brain, load Mouth. Saved 3.8 MB, got total down to 12 MB.

Used a trick where the middleware generates silence instead of storing silent audio files. Ambience tracks with 5 seconds of silence used to cost 500 KB - now 100 KB. 80% smaller.

Wrote C# scripts that detect which zone you're in and tell Wwise to swap audio banks. Made a waterfall with 3 sound layers that crossfade based on distance. Footsteps check what surface you're on and switch sounds automatically.

What went wrong:

Only tested on Android emulator, not actual phones.

Emulator reported 12 MB but real hardware might be different - background apps, OS overhead, device-specific quirks.

Would need to test on actual cheap Android devices with profiling tools before shipping.

What I Learned

Skills from fixing problems when they broke

Performance Limits

Working within sound channel limits, tracking memory budgets, picking compression that balances file size vs quality.

3D Audio

Writing distance curves for different sound types, positioning audio in 3D space, making sounds fade at the right ranges.

Middleware Integration

Connecting game events to middleware, making audio respond to player position and game state without hardcoding values.

Memory Management

Deciding what loads immediately, what streams from disk, what unloads when changing zones. Staying under budget on phones.

Unity + Wwise

Writing C# scripts that talk to Wwise API, handling zone triggers, cleaning up sounds that don't stop properly.

Multi-Platform

Different compression strategies for PC vs mobile. Testing on emulators. Profiling memory to catch issues before shipping.

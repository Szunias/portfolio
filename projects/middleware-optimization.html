Learning Wwise Through Problems

Three school projects where I ran into real technical walls and had to figure out how to get past them. Managing hundreds of simultaneous sounds without everything cutting out. Making audio adapt smoothly between game zones. Fitting an entire game's audio into 15 MB of RAM on a phone.

These are solo projects from Digital Arts & Entertainment (2021-2024). Not shipped games, but they forced me to solve the same kinds of problems you'd hit in production - performance budgets, memory constraints, platform differences, middleware integration.

Boney Boogaloo

Wwise v2021 Windows 2 weeks

You're building audio for a zombie wave shooter. Fifty zombies spawn on screen at once, all groaning and shuffling. The player is firing constantly - gunshots, reloads, shell casings hitting the floor. There's ambient music, environment sounds, and UI feedback happening simultaneously.

Then you hit play and half the sounds cut out. The audio engine can only handle 256 simultaneous voices. Go over that and Wwise starts killing sounds to stay under budget. Important gameplay audio - zombie spawns, low ammo warnings - gets culled because ambient loops are hogging voices.

You need every sound under 256 voices, but you also can't have the same gunshot loop 100 times. That sounds mechanical and breaks immersion. The challenge: stay under budget while keeping variation.

What I Built
10 random containers
44 audio files total
256 voice limit
6 gunshot variations

The solution: Random containers for everything that repeats. Each gunshot pulls from a pool of six variations. Zombie sounds randomize between groans, shuffles, and growls. I set up voice virtualization so quiet background sounds pause instead of stopping completely - they save their voice for when they're audible again.

Then I hand-trimmed silence from every file. That half-second of dead air at the start of a reload? Cut. The tail on a zombie groan that's just room noise? Gone. Smaller files load faster and take less memory.

What Didn't Work

I set the culling threshold at -80 dB thinking "quiet enough = safe to cut." But zombie spawn sounds were getting culled mid-wave because they started quiet and ramped up. Players couldn't hear new threats spawning behind them. Had to raise the threshold to -60 dB and manually rebalance all the volume levels instead of relying on automatic culling.

Ellen

Wwise v2021 Windows 3 weeks

Third-person action game with three distinct zones: dense jungle, echoing caves, and concrete buildings. You're moving between them constantly during combat. The problem: when you step from jungle into a building, the audio cuts instantly. Jungle ambience stops, building ambience starts. It feels jarring and unpolished.

There's also a spatial problem. Enemy footsteps need different ranges depending on the threat. A grenadier throwing explosives from 75 meters away? You need to hear those footsteps for situational awareness. But debris falling off a crate 10 meters behind you? That should fade fast or it clutters the mix.

What I Built
6.5s zone crossfades
10 attenuation curves
4 reverb channels
11m - 155m distance range

The solution: State-driven ambience layers. Wwise tracks which zone you're in (jungle, cave, building) and automatically crossfades the ambience over 6.5 seconds. Walk into a building and the jungle sounds gradually fade out while room tone fades in. No abrupt cuts, no manual scripting.

For spatial audio, I wrote ten custom attenuation curves. Debris cuts off at 11 meters. Gates and large mechanical sounds reach 155 meters. Grenadier footsteps sit at 75 meters for threat awareness. Each sound has a purpose-built falloff that matches its gameplay importance.

I added four reverb channels (Cave, StoreRoom, Stone Big, Stone Small) controlled by real-time game parameters. When you enter a cave, the game sends a value to Wwise and reverb increases automatically. I can tweak reverb amounts in Wwise without recompiling the game.

What I Learned

This was my first time writing attenuation curves from scratch. Initial attempt: too aggressive. Enemies went silent at 30 meters and players complained they couldn't hear threats approaching. Second attempt: too conservative. Everything was audible at 100+ meters and the mix turned into mush. Took four playtests to find the sweet spot - start conservative, iterate based on actual gameplay, not guesswork.

Stomach Aches

Wwise 2024 4 Platforms 3 weeks

FPS platformer set inside a human body. Three massive zones: Brain, Mouth, Stomach. You're jumping between them constantly. The game needs to run on Windows, Mac, and low-end Android phones with 2GB of RAM.

The problem: Your audio memory budget on Android_Low is 15 MB maximum. Load all three zones at once and you're sitting at 25+ MB. The game crashes on launch. You can't ship this.

Windows can handle uncompressed audio, but Android needs aggressive compression. You need different builds for different platforms, and they all need to play the same content.

What I Built
12 MB runtime memory
3.8 MB saved via loading
80% silence reduction
4 platforms

The solution: Dynamic soundbank loading. Only load audio for the zone you're currently in. Start in Brain? Load Brain audio. Enter Mouth? Unload Brain, load Mouth. This saved 3.8 MB of RAM and brought total usage down to ~12 MB.

I used Wwise's Silence plugin for ambience gaps. Instead of storing silent WAV files (which still consume memory), the plugin generates procedural silence. An ambience track with five seconds of silence used to cost 500 KB. Now it costs 100 KB. That's 80% memory reduction on gap-heavy tracks.

I wrote C# scripts in Unity to handle zone triggers and distance-based mixing. Walk toward the waterfall and three audio layers crossfade based on your distance - splashes up close, mid-range water flow, distant rumble. Footsteps raycast downward and switch between gut tissue, tongue, and skull bone materials automatically.

Limitations

Only tested on Android emulator, not real hardware. Emulator reported 12 MB usage, but real phones might behave differently due to background processes, OS overhead, or hardware-specific memory management. Would need to profile on actual devices with varying specs before calling this production-ready.

What I Know Now

Technical skills from hitting walls and figuring out how to fix them

🎯 Performance Tuning

Working within voice limits, setting memory budgets, choosing compression formats that balance quality against file size.

🔊 3D Audio

Writing attenuation curves for different sound types, positioning audio in 3D space, setting cone angles for directional sounds.

⚙️ Middleware Integration

Connecting game events to Wwise through states, switches, and real-time parameters. Making audio respond to gameplay without hardcoding.

📦 Memory Management

Deciding what loads immediately, what streams from disk, what unloads when you change zones. Staying under budget on memory-constrained platforms.

🔗 Unity + Wwise

Writing C# scripts that talk to Wwise, managing playback IDs, cleaning up audio voices to prevent leaks.

📱 Platform Optimization

Different compression strategies for PC versus mobile. Testing on emulators. Profiling memory usage to catch problems before they ship.

Boney Boogaloo

Wwise v2021 Windows 2 weeks

Zombie shooter where 50+ enemies spawn at once. The audio engine can only play 256 sounds simultaneously - go over that and important sounds cut out. I needed to keep it under budget while making sure gunshots and zombie sounds don't get repetitive.

The Problem

Too Many Sounds

More than 256 simultaneous sounds = audio cuts out

Repetitive Audio

Same gunshot 100 times sounds mechanical

What I Did

Voice Virtualization

Quiet sounds pause instead of stopping - saves voices for important stuff

Random Pools

10 containers, 44 files total - each gunshot picks randomly from 6 variations

Manual Trimming

Cut silence from every file to reduce memory and load times

What didn't work:

Initially tried -80 dB culling threshold but it cut off zombie spawns too early. Had to adjust to -60 dB and manually balance volumes instead.

Random Containers Voice Virtualization ADPCM (audio codec)

Ellen

Wwise v2021 Windows 3 weeks

Third-person action game with jungle, cave, and building zones. When you move between areas, the ambience needs to change without jarring cuts. Enemy footsteps need different audio ranges - close enemies for threat awareness, distant ones fade earlier.

The Problem

Abrupt Zone Changes

Audio cutting when entering buildings feels unpolished

Distance Balance

Grenadier footsteps at 75m for awareness, but debris should cut at 11m

What I Did

State Crossfades

6.5 second fades between zones - Wwise handles transitions automatically

10 Attenuation Curves

Different ranges for different sounds: 11m for debris, 155m for gates

Real-time Reverb

4 reverb channels controlled by game - I can adjust without recompiling

What I learned:

First time writing attenuation curves from scratch. Took multiple playtests to find the right balance - enemies were either too quiet or too loud. Learned to start conservative and iterate.

State System RTPC (real-time controls) 3D Positioning Material Footsteps

Stomach Aches

Wwise 2024 4 Platforms 3 weeks

FPS platformer inside a human body. Needed to run on Windows, Mac, and low-end Android phones with 2GB RAM. Target: keep audio memory under 15 MB on Android_Low. The game has three large zones (Brain, Mouth, Stomach) - loading all audio at once would blow the budget.

The Problem

RAM Budget

15 MB max on Android_Low - all zones loaded = 25+ MB

Platform Differences

Windows handles uncompressed, Android needs aggressive compression

What I Did

Zone Loading

Load Brain audio, unload it when entering Mouth - saves 3.8 MB

Procedural Silence

Generate silence in Wwise instead of storing WAV files - 80% smaller

C# Integration

Wrote Unity scripts to handle zone triggers and distance-based mixing

Limitations:

Only tested on Android emulator, not real device. Emulator showed 12 MB usage but real hardware might be different. Would want to verify on actual phones with profiling tools before calling this production-ready.

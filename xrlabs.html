What I Do Here

XR Labs is a Belgian initiative where defence, federal police, and customs build VR/AR training simulations together. I lead the technical sound design - building audio systems that make virtual training scenarios feel real enough to be useful.

The people using these simulations are training for situations where audio matters. Footsteps behind a door, radio chatter, weapon handling sounds, environmental cues - if it doesn't sound right, the training doesn't work. My job is making sure the spatial audio is accurate enough that trainees react the same way they would in the field.

I work with Unity and Steam Audio, building real-time audio systems that respond to player actions and environment changes. Everything runs locally. The specifics of what we build are confidential, but the core challenge is the same one I face in every project: make the audio invisible. When nobody notices the sound, it means everyone trusts it.

Focus Spatial audio for VR training environments
End Users Belgian Defence, Federal Police, Customs
Pipeline Unity + Steam Audio, version-controlled with Perforce
Why It Matters If the audio is wrong, trainees don't react naturally. The simulation fails.
